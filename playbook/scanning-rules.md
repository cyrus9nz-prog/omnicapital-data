## DEVELOPED RULES

**R1 — Three-layer macro scan. [confidence: high]**
Run every macro disruption to L3. Direct → adjacent → behavioural. Origin: a 400% run
in car rentals was missed after a conflict-driven scan covered energy and defence and
stopped. The behavioural leg — what people do instead when the normal channel is
disrupted, and who captures that demand — is systematically underscanned. Now Method
step 3 in the SKILL.

**R2 — Retail sentiment is a mandatory input, not optional colour. [confidence: medium]**
Search the forums on every scan. It improves timing and momentum identification. It does
not improve fundamental accuracy. Use as a confirmation layer; blind trust in it is how
people buy the top of a squeeze. Divergence between sentiment and price is the useful
signal, not sentiment level.

**R3 — Sponsor distribution overrides narrative quality. [confidence: high, bounded]**
When the entity that created a vehicle is selling into retail demand at scale, the
thesis is compromised regardless of the underlying assets. Origin: a Form 144 flipped
the single most attractive narrative of the session to Avoid. Generalises to CEFs and
SPAC-like wrappers. Does not generalise to ordinary insider sales at operating companies.

**R4 — Prefer the direct equity claim over the wrapper. [confidence: medium]**
Between vehicles offering exposure to the same private asset, prefer the one holding an
actual equity claim over one carrying premium-to-NAV or liquidity risk. Relative
judgment, revisit if deal terms change.

**R5 — Small fixed budget: constrain by structure, not by conviction. [confidence: medium]**
For a fixed lotto budget: cap each position at half the budget; minimum ~6–8 weeks to
expiry so the thesis has room; sell half on a double; mental 50% stop. Derived from
first principles for a specific constraint, not backtested. Treat the constraint as an
optimisation problem, not a handicap.

**R6 — Contrarian-media signals are folklore. [confidence: low — logged, weight ≈ 0]**
A pundit dismissal of a retail-driven small cap is not a buy indicator. Recorded because
it was said in session, not because it should be used.

## THE BUY-NOW vs WAIT DECISION — UNRESOLVED

Flagged in the gap audit and still unsolved. Three "wait" calls produced three different
outcomes: one protected capital, one missed the move entirely, one was really a sizing
call in disguise. No decision tree exists — in practice "wait" gets issued when the
entry price feels uncomfortable relative to a recent run, which is a description of the
output, not a rule.

Until this is resolved, Gate 7 is the stopgap: a pullback level requires a named
mechanism, a catalyst, or a time window, and otherwise the answer is scale in thirds.
Resolving it properly needs 8–10 logged "wait" decisions with outcomes. That is an
evals problem, not a prompt problem.
