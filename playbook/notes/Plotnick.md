# Plotnick — Fundamentals for Profit in Undervalued Stocks (1964) — notes
## chunk 1 (p.1–29)
- p.21 Definition: "Undervalued" = depressed price + strong asset protection + strong signs of change for the better in earnings outlook. Evidence: case (Budget Finance 1961-62 $8->$16; broker recommended at $10) — C, hindsight.
- p.22 Most important element = change in earnings. Case Heywood Wakefield $13 Jul 1961 -> $24 Mar 1962 — C.
- p.23 Low prices are not accidents; long declines create emotional block (S.H. Kress $60 1946 -> $18). Three outcomes of a loss-making cheap stock: liquidation, breakeven drift, return to strong earnings (Standard Fruit case). — principle D/C.
- p.24-25 Dangers: low price itself a danger signal; line between survival and failure "as thin as a short-term liability"; preserve capital first. — principle D.
- p.11-13 intro: claims Undervalueds' price movements forecastable "with a surprisingly high degree of success" — author claims, no test.
## chunk 2 (p.30–43)
- p.32 Danger of inadequate information: a holder of a company reporting only annually can't act; price drift down reflects leakage of bad results. Principle: prefer companies with quarterly (interim) reports; "risks increase in proportion to a decrease in available information" (p.34). Hypothetical X Co case — D.
- p.33-34 Listed vs OTC: listed companies must report interim; check reporting frequency before buying. Dated: 1964 OTC disclosure regime (now SEC quarterly reporting for registrants). Principle D.
- p.34-35 Conrad Carson case: loss, weak net current assets, no current info -> $6 -> $1 (−83%). C.
- p.34 Sources used: S&P Monthly Stock Guide for first step; Moody's Industrials later. Dated sources -> modern equivalents FMP/10-K.
- p.37 Analyst technique: inventory-to-next-half-sales ratio as sales forecast; projected earnings = stable net margin (5–8 years) × projected sales. Process idea, D.
- p.41-43 Liberty Fabrics case: purchase timing problem, discounting = buying in anticipation of future earnings. C (hindsight).
## chunk 3 (p.44–58)
- p.44-45 Discounting: prices move on anticipated earnings; "sell the news" (Ford split p.45), overdiscounting (Polaroid ~105x earnings -> $81, p.44-45). Principle C.
- p.46-47 Undervalueds sit at or near historic price lows or very low P/E; less discounting. D.
- p.47 SELL RULE: "The only reason for sale is faltering performance or reversal of performance by the company"; or price far out of proportion to present and future performance. Don't grab small profits. D/C.
- p.48 SELL RULE detail: hold while quarterly earnings rate maintained/improves; e.g. Q1 EPS $1.20, price $12 -> still undervalued vs annualised; IF a quarterly report shows decline in earnings, sales or profit margins -> sell signal. Cases Beech-Nut (hold), General Instrument (sell; declines in sales, earnings, margins + high P/E), Standard Fruit. C.
- p.48-49 Sell on price/judgment: big fast run (Liberty Fabrics +80% in 3 months), small untried co., market bearish -> sell when it starts to drift back. Judgment, principle D.
- p.49-50 Sell immediately on adverse new info even a week after purchase. Principle D. "buy at the low usually expensive"; act on decision.
- p.50-51 High P/E (Revlon >40x, slow growth) -> vulnerability; when serious doubts exist take stock out. C. Any significant slowdown/reversal in performance data = sell signal (p.51).
- p.55 Two legs of price gain: improved earnings + rising multiplier. D.
- p.56 Old rule: 10x earnings; DJIA 6x 1949 vs 23x 1961 (dated). Republic Aviation: low P/E (<7x) on earnings that will not continue (main product phasing out, no replacement) -> not a buy; fell <$20. C. Continuity of earnings matters.
- p.57 glamour cycles (TV, electronics, supermarkets, discount stores). Principle.
- p.58 Charts: tool not machine; breakout above accumulation level described; don't rely only on price/volume. Principle D.
## chunk 4 (p.59–72)
- p.59-60 Buy on change in fundamentals: Lockheed loss -> profits, bought at 11x projected EPS -> rose; United Fruit new mgmt -> earnings. C.
- p.61 Sell on weakening fundamentals: falling ad revenue (ABC), steel margins eroding + dividend barely covered by earnings (Bethlehem) + high P/E (US Steel 83x... -> 16x). "No stock too big to stumble", stumble violent when multiplier high. C. Dividend barely covered = signal of trouble (agrees R-138?).
- p.62 Protection layers: low P/E, strong current assets, solid total assets, management that held costs while raising sales/margins. Liquidometer: deficit -> profit in H1; net current assets ~ price, total assets ~2x price; survived since 1928. C.
- p.63 Dunn Engineering: no protection, marginal finances -> bankruptcy. "protection against bankruptcy lies in book value, working capital, net current asset value minus long-term debt". C. Speculator may buy on promise; investor waits for promise to become reality (p.64).
- p.64-65 Bull/bear: 40–50x P/E can't be sustained; bear markets decapitate high-P/E stocks. Legislative/tax changes as sell signals (S&L tax). Principle D.
- p.66 Multiplier rule: buy at a low multiple of current earnings with projected higher earnings; target = same multiple × projected EPS (12x $2 -> 12x $2.75 = $33); don't count on multiple expansion. 3M at 40x: downside multiple risk. D (worked hypothetical).
- p.71 Analyst looks for: earning money, reasonable price, strong indications of substantially improved earnings, strong financial/asset condition, efficient management; sell on faltering. D.
- p.72 Method: staged investigation with early cut-off points when danger signals appear. Process principle.
## chunk 5 (p.73–87)
- p.73 Process: use a public corporate announcement of a "change" as the screening trigger ("signal"), then first look (5 surface steps; stop immediately if price high vs earnings), second look (6 steps, 2 years operations), third look (18 steps, 4-year record; analysts prefer 10). Staged elimination. D. Inputs: 8-K/press releases.
- p.74 Third look used also to evaluate current holdings; projections of earnings and probable market reaction.
- p.80 Signal's content irrelevant at first; it just directs attention; weigh meaning only when the change is accomplished fact and after third look (Bon Ami class B elimination). D/C.
- p.81 Five signal areas: plant/costs, product, management, capitalization, miscellaneous (govt, suits, R&D). Cost cutting with static sales as good as sales growth.
- p.82 Wage settlement: compare increase vs prior margins; no price increase/no labour cuts -> margins narrow. D.
- p.83 New product signals: "many developments fail to develop" (Technicolor 16->38->13). Mergers: acquired co often better buy; acquisitions outside field = risk; require sound acquirer finances. C.
- p.84 Management change signal (Chrysler) — measured by subsequent sales, costs, earnings. D.
- p.85-86 Government involvement (suits, investigations, antitrust) -> "strong signal to stay away"; litigation settlement may create an Undervalued. C/D. Dated (1960s antitrust).
- p.87 Invest in fundamentals not press releases; R&D signals rarely succeed. Principle D.
## chunk 6 (p.88–104)
- p.91-92 FIRST LOOK step 2: earnings for current period + two most recent full years -> trend; direction as well as amount. Candidates: loss-maker with significant declines in losses; static earner with signs of substantial improvement. D.
- p.93 No arbitrary P/E limits — industry/company specific; buyer-of-business analogy (5x earnings). Nonrecurring income: see appendix (strip extraordinary items, p.94 American Photocopy 1959 included nonrecurring capital gains).
- p.94 Buy at the low end of a stock's historic P/E band (Jersey Std 12–15x: buy at 12, not 15); earnings gain + constant multiple = gain; multiple expansion = bonus. High P/E: multiple compression risk. D (worked example).
- p.95 Growth premium: price vs earnings projected 3 years at historic growth rate. 18x with growth to 25x may be good value; 8x static poor value. Buy on merits, not industry P/E.
- p.95-96 Shares outstanding: thin float -> volatility and no fair market (caution); per-share base. "Floating supply" appendix.
- p.96-97 Step 5 NCAV per share less long-term debt (current assets − current liabilities − LTD)/shares. Survival test. Don't buy if NCAV pennies vs price (Dynaelectron 16c vs $2–8; ratio ~1:1). C.
- p.98 Current ratio < 1 = danger sign (rule of thumb); Standard Screw 3.7:1 healthy. D.
- p.98 First-look discard rule: trend down or level AND price rich vs earnings -> discard; else compute NCAV-LTD; if it "shows some muscle" -> second look.
- p.99 Worked example Standard Screw: price 23, EPS 1.24->2.00 (+60%), P/E 11.5, NCAV−LTD $14.43/share ~ approaching price -> promising. Thin OTC float = caution. C (case).
- p.104 Second look: sales, COGS, gross profit, cost of sales (SG&A), net profit, book value (net tangible asset value/share); 2 full years + current. Financial means first, products later.
## chunk 7 (p.105–118)
- p.105 Shrinking margins + slowing growth = warning even for a quality co (American Home Products 1962). C.
- p.105 Sales: acquisitions inflate sales; uptrend in sales prime requisite for earnings gains; cost-cutting has a floor.
- p.106 SECOND LOOK rule: sales off sharply -> stop; flat/slightly up -> continue; ideal strong sales uptrend with other elements same direction.
- p.107-109 Compute COGS/sales, gross margin, SG&A/sales, pretax net margin for 2 years (Western Tablet: COGS 87.6%->85.7%, gross 12.4->14.3%, SG&A 4.0->4.3%, pretax margin 8.3->9.9% on +4% sales). C (worked case).
- p.109 SG&A as gauge of management efficiency; ad spend that doesn't pay -> declining earnings even with rising sales (Eldon). C.
- p.110 Growth: 20% growth -> premium (IBM); economy grows 4% (dated).
- p.110-111 Book value (net tangible asset value/share) = safety line, not a price driver; below book doesn't mean buy (Curtiss Wright book $25 vs $16). C.
- p.111-112 SECOND LOOK discard rule: declining sales AND narrowing margins -> stop ("immediately preclude further investigation"); if sales up sharply and margins slightly narrower -> give benefit of doubt (start-up costs, losing division drag). If sales down/static, margins down, costs up -> discontinue. D.
- p.112-113 BOOK VALUE requirement scales with risk: loss-maker showing recovery -> book value per share ABOVE current price is a prerequisite; erratic performer -> book value close to price; steady improver -> book < 50% of price adequate. Ideal: book double price. D (no test). (no arbitrary 75% limit).
- p.117 Third look: 4-year record minimum (6–10 desirable) + current period; corporate history.
- p.118 Third-look 13 items: working capital, total/current/fixed assets, current/LT liabilities, shares, earnings, price range, P/E, book value/share, sales, COGS, gross profit, S&A expense, net profit.
## chunk 8 (p.119–133)
- p.119 Working capital: adequate and growing over 4 years.
- p.119 Asset adjustments: deduct goodwill/intangibles carried above nominal from asset totals (NCAV−LTD and book per share); haircut specialised/illiquid inventories to ~50% when inventories dominate current assets; strange non-core assets = possible danger; 40% drop in current assets with no fixed-asset rise or loss -> find out why (inventory method change, write-off). D. (appendix Inventories)
- p.120 Loss-making company: mentally mark down fixed assets for forced sale.
- p.121 Liability warning signs: short-term bank loans; large increase in accounts payable (stretching creditors). D.
- p.121-122 Check share count changes (splits) when comparing EPS.
- p.122 Four-year earnings: two-year recovery can look weak on 4 years.
- p.122 PRICE-RANGE TARGET: e.g. 1957 range $19–31 on EPS $2 (10–15x); 1961 price $6 with return to $2 EPS -> expect at least $19 (10x = low end of historic multiple). D (hypothetical).
- p.122-123 Average P/E per year = (high+low)/2 / EPS; compare current vs 4-year history to judge depressed vs high. D.
- p.123 Losses erode net tangible asset value per share.
- p.123-124 Sales trend momentum both ways; erratic sales (up/down) = very dangerous -> 4-year review necessary. D.
- p.124 Ideal: cost control, margins maintained/improved with rising sales over 4 years. Dividends and cash flow (NI + depreciation) acknowledged but de-emphasised for Undervalueds (p.124).
- p.125 Apply tests at purchase and continuously while held; sell when a downtrend starts.
- p.129-133 McCall case (1958): signal = director changes; price 13–13.5; 9m EPS 1.51 vs 1.24 (+22%); 1956 1.16, 1955 1.74. Q4 1956 loss (9m 1.24 > FY 1.16) -> don't annualise 9m earnings when prior Q4 was weak; project FY = 9m figure (p.130-131). P/E ~9x on that. Shares 610k thin. NCAV−LTD $8.60 (~2/3–3/4 of price). Sales +13% 1956 but EPS down; COGS 73.4–73.5% stable; SG&A 18.1%->19.4%. C (case).
- Seasonality rule (p.130-131): when last year's Q4 was weak/loss, don't annualise YTD earnings; use YTD as FY estimate. D/C.

## chunk 11 (p.167–180)
- p.167 Aldens: pretax margin 4.4% highest in 4 years; near 4-year low; mean P/E 5,6,11,8; book ~2x price. Needed patience (flat 6 months). Rose to ~$90 (~4x). C.
- p.168 SELL: annual EPS flattened (2.09 vs 2.07) -> sell; stock fell 80 -> 62. C. Held while margins and sales improving.
- p.171-172 Selling attitude: don't hold losers to "get even"; opportunity cost; don't fear selling then seeing a rise. Principle D.
- p.173 Typical Undervalued needs ~2 years to come to fruition (author estimate, D). Think of buying the corporation.
- p.173-174 SELL REVIEW at each earnings report: sales, COGS, gross profit, SG&A, net profit + price, EPS, P/E; trends have momentum — a down quarter in sales or margin is unlikely to reverse next quarter -> change downward in earnings direction = sell signal. D.
- p.174 Interim example: sales +7%, EPS 64c->28c, margin 6%->3.5% -> sell signal.
- p.174-175 Seasonality: compare quarter with same quarter prior year, then vs prior full year; weak seasonal quarter still valid unless nonrecurring item. D.
- p.175 Industry trend: industry adversity (steel, S&L 1962) is a valid sell signal even if company is fine. D.
- p.175-178 FLAT EARNINGS: flat sales and flat margins -> price stays flat or drifts lower; sell (opportunity cost) unless evidence of change; F.G. Shattuck 1952–57 flat while DJIA doubled; then earnings rose -> price rose; flattened again -> fell. C. "When earnings are flat, price remains flat until evidence of strong improvement in the foreseeable future—one year" (p.178).
- p.178 Cyclicals: "sell when the earnings improvement starts to decline". Most stocks are cyclical. D.
- p.179 Howe Sound 10-year round trip; "put them away, but don't forget them". C. Cyclical stock suits the method: financially sound co. in weak cycle can recover. D.
- p.179-180 DECLINING EARNINGS: change downward in earnings trend (declining sales + higher costs) = definite sell signal; American Bakeries strong balance sheet, no debt, current ratio ~4, but EPS 3.49->1.02 over 6 years, price $50->$22 — balance-sheet strength doesn't save a declining earner. C.
