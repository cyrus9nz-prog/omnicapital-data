## loc.14–26
- loc.14: author's criteria for a strategy: few clear rules, decision in seconds, any account size ($2k up), ~15 min/day. Principle. D.
- loc.15: a stock that has ranged for months is more likely to keep ranging; once trending, more likely to keep trending (assertion; one chart). D.
- loc.16: find stocks that are starting to move; author asserts moves that start tend to continue; examples: downtrend → sideways → breakout up. Author sells his own scanner. D/C.
- loc.17: GOLDEN RULE risk $1 to make $2 (agrees R-010). 2% RULE: never risk more than 2% of the account per trade, especially when starting or small (agrees R-009). Hypothetical $5,000 account: 10 straight losses at $100 leave $4,000. D.
- loc.18–19: take profits while they are there; IPO examples where holders rode a first-day pop and later fell well below (named stocks; cases only) → C for "take profits at target". 
- loc.19–20: target is exactly 2× the risk ("not three, not four, not five"); once hit, exit and stop watching; with 1:2 you can be wrong half the time and still make money (arithmetic: 5×$200 − 5×$100 = $500). Exits matter more than entries; place stop and target orders at entry (bracket) so the trade runs on autopilot. D (arithmetic, not a test).
- loc.21–24 INDICATORS: daily OHLC bars; Slow Stochastic 14,3,3; RSI 7; MACD 12,26,9. Uptrend reading: %K above 50 and rising; RSI above 50 and rising; MACD above its signal line and rising. Downtrend: all three the opposite. All three up = green bar; all three down = red bar; mixed = black bar (sideways). Author's experience: three indicators best (fewer → false signals, more → conflicts). C/D.
- loc.22: author claims one example stock would have made 61% with PowerX vs 16% buy-and-hold over 12 months (single chart example) → "author claims", C.
- loc.25–26 ENTRY: buy stop $0.01 above the high of the FIRST green bar; leave the order while bars stay green; cancel if a black bar prints and wait for the next green bar. Short: sell stop $0.01 below the low of the first red bar (or buy puts). Only the FIRST signal in a trend; never chase; if missed, find another stock (agrees R-023). → core RULE. Evidence: chart examples only → C.
## loc.27–39
- loc.27: decide the exit before entry; three exits always: stop, target, black-bar exit.
- loc.27–28 STOP: ADR = average of (daily high − daily low) over the last 7 trading days; stop = entry − 1.5 × ADR (long). For option trades, don't place the stop order on the option: watch the stock and close the option manually when the STOCK breaks the stop level, because option quotes are wider. → part of core RULE.
- loc.29–30 TARGET: target = entry + 3 × ADR (2:1 vs the stop); place the target order right after entry. Author: "from working with the strategy in many market environments" it works best at 2:1 on ADR — experience, no test shown → C. Arithmetic: 5 × $126 − 5 × $63 = $315.
- loc.30–31 BLACK-BAR EXIT: when the indicators disagree (black bar), close at the next open. One example: exit $8.85 instead of the $7.73 stop. C.
- loc.31: author asserts lower-priced stocks make larger % moves, so his examples are low-priced; he takes trades at any price (tension with R-027's low-priced exclusion). D.
- loc.32–33: options pros (small capital, limited risk if done right, leverage); most options that expire worthless are long shots needing big fast moves. D.
- loc.33: choose shares vs options by account size, experience, stock price and the liquidity of both the stock and its options. D.
- loc.35–37: intrinsic/extrinsic; at expiry only intrinsic remains; decay speeds up near expiry. OPTION EXPIRY: buy 30–45 days to expiry although trades are held ~10–20 days, to avoid the fast decay of the final ~3 weeks. → part of options RULE. C/D (author's experience).
- loc.38: keep it simple: long → buy a call; short → buy a put; master these before spreads. Contract = 100 shares. D.
- loc.39: example: stock buy $11.10, ADR $0.34, stop $10.59, target $12.12 hit in days (+$102/100 sh, 9%); $10-strike call, 45 days, $1.20 → $2.22 (+85%). Single example → C.
## loc.40–52
- loc.40–43 OPTION CHOICE: after a stock buy signal, buy a CALL (put for shorts) ~40 days out; author never trades OTM (long shots), prefers ITM (holds value, tracks the stock). His software's estimated values at target and stop: ITM 1:1.7, ATM 1:1.6, OTM 1:1.2 reward:risk → ITM best. Enter the option only after the stock trades above the signal-bar high. Exit the option on the STOCK's signals (stop, target, black bar); check after the close. → options RULE. Evidence: one example with the vendor software's estimated option values → C. Agrees with Cayman R-034 (strike at or in the money). Note ITM 1.7:1 sits below R-010's 2.
- loc.43: author claims his money management "potentially" turns $5,000 into $41,400 in 12 months (projection, not a result).
- loc.44: rarely more than 5% of trading funds in any one trade (agrees R-040). Don't trade too aggressively early; don't stay a one-lot trader forever.
- loc.45 RISK STEP-UP: start with 2% risk per trade; once proven, risk more ("you have to violate the 2% rule" to grow) — only after making money at least 4 weeks in a row; when you lose money, cut risk per trade (anti-martingale). → candidate RULE (risk/sizing), conflicts R-009. D (author's experience).
- loc.45–46: Larry Williams quote: his contest gain came mostly from aggressive sizing (anecdote, C). Ralph Vince experiment: 40 PhDs, $1,000, 100 trades, 60% win rate, even payoff; only 2 of 40 finished with a profit (source given: CSI News Journal, March 1992) → B. Lesson: sizing and gambler's-fallacy behaviour after losses can sink a positive-edge game.
- loc.47–49 FIXED RATIO (author's favourite): start at 2% risk ($200 on $10,000) and a weekly goal ($100); after 4 weeks of hitting it (equity +$400 → $10,400) raise risk by $200 to $400; next step at $11,200 (+$800) to $600; then $12,400 (+$1,200) to $800 — each step needs equity to grow by 2× the current risk (my reading of the book's table steps). Projection: $10k → $52k in 12 months, $86k in 18 (hypothetical, "could potentially"). Optimal F (Vince) needs detailed strategy stats; percent-risk is safe but slow. → part of risk step-up RULE. D (projection).
- loc.50–51: simulator: set it to the real account size and trade the plan; after 4 consistently profitable weeks, start small with real money (agrees demo-account principle). D.
- loc.51–52: an underfunded account breeds fear and gambling: 10 losses of $100 is −20% on $5,000 but −50% on $2,000; author lost half of $20,000 early and froze. C/D.
- loc.52: execute the plan (a plan you don't act on earns nothing). D.

- loc.54 PAPER GATE: before real money, place at least 40 round-trip simulator trades with the one strategy (author calls 40 "statistically relevant" — assertion) AND be profitable in the simulator for 4 weeks. → candidate RULE (process). D.
