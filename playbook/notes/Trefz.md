- Front matter: EBS doctoral thesis 2022 (Springer 2023). Three essays: (1) REIT-specific Fama-French factors + short interest 1999–2019; (2) Covid-19 sector performance/spillovers; (3) ESG REITs in Covid. Lists of figures/tables only.
## chunk p.15–27
- p.17–18: REITs reflect underlying property long run; REIT-specific factors (equal-weight REIT market, size, dividend-yield value factor) beat plain Fama-French for REIT returns; value factor built on dividend yield since REITs pay out 90% of income and mean-revert to NAV (p.18) — method, not a trading rule.
- p.22–24: literature on short interest in REITs conflicting: heavily shorted REITs underperform (French et al. 2005–07); high short interest + low transparency → overvaluation (Chen et al. 1990–2005); short activity explains ~1/3 of NAV premium variation (Brounen et al. 2006–08) (p.23) — B (cited studies). Authors: with REIT-specific factors, an apparent short-interest effect for large REITs is an omitted-variable artefact; size doesn't matter; short factor only touches neutral-yield portfolios (p.24) — A-type claim (results later).
- p.24–27 data: FTSE NAREIT All REITs constituents Jan 1999–Dec 2019 (survivorship controlled), Bloomberg monthly; short interest ratio = days to cover (short interest ÷ avg daily volume) (p.24); Table 1 p.26: ~113–185 equity REITs per year; median equity REIT days-to-cover 1.3 (1999) to ~8 (2007–08), ~4 in 2019. Monthly rebalancing (p.26–27).
## chunk p.28–44
- p.28–29: factor construction: size split 50/50 by market cap; value = dividend-yield terciles (high yield = value, low yield = prime/growth); short-interest factor = low minus high days-to-cover, size-controlled.
- p.30–33 (Tables 2–4 p.34–36): REIT-specific 3-factor model adj R² 0.88–0.98 vs plain Fama-French ≤0.51; REITs load ~0 or slightly negative on stock-market factor in combined model → treat REITs as their own sector; plain FF factors inadequate for REIT risk adjustment (A — 1999–2019 monthly, ~113–185 equity REITs).
- p.36–38, Table 6 p.40: short-interest quintiles (monthly sort on days-to-cover, equal weight, 1-month hold): LOW short-interest quintile has positive significant alpha ≈0.3%/month (p=0.02) under REIT-specific model; high quintile alpha ~0; FF model gives 0.6%/month for low (Table 7 p.41). Agrees with Chen et al. 2012, Li & Yung 2004 (p.38). → candidate RULE (value? or growth/momentum? — sentiment/short-interest: put under value as screen? choose "value"? Better "swing"? No — monthly factor; school: value?). In-sample, before costs. Grade A.
- p.41–44 Tables 8–9: adding short-interest factor: with REIT-specific factors it is significant only for neutral-yield portfolios (negative loadings), size doesn't matter; with plain FF it looks like large REITs (artefact of omitted variables) (p.44).

## chunk p.116–138
- p.116–122 ESG paper: high vs low ESG (top/bottom half by S&P Global ESG score), weekly, value-weighted; first Covid wave. Returns: no significant difference under any adjustment (t-test p 0.72–0.97, Table 56 p.122); rolling 23-week t-tests 2018–2020 show no consistent return gap (p.131). Contradicts Albuquerque et al. (p.123). No ESG alpha before or during.
- p.123–127: after full stock + RE adjustment, high-ESG portfolio had significantly lower GARCH volatility in the crisis (F-test p<0.01, Table 58 p.127); not significant before Covid (rolling F-tests p.134–137). VaR also smaller for high ESG (Table 59 p.130).
- p.138 conclusion: ESG doesn't hurt returns; lowers risk in crisis after proper adjustment; authors back ESG-promoting regulation (policy view — not carried).
- Candidate: principle (risk/sizing context): within REITs, high-ESG halves showed lower volatility/VaR in one crisis, no return edge — graded A for the test (weekly, ~2.5y, one episode) but not a rule (no return edge; ESG score source paid).
