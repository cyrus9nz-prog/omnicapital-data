# Fridson — The Little Book of Picking Top Stocks (2023) — reading notes
## loc.1–12
- Front matter, foreword. Book's aim: identify the year's #1 total-return stock in the S&P 500; study period 2012–2021 (Table P.1 #1 stocks, returns 80%–743%, source Bloomberg) loc.4, 11.
- Principle: speculative "one big win" bets should be a small slice; author says Siegel's 10–15% is much more than necessary (implies ~1–2% for speculation) loc.8 — D (cites Siegel quote).
- Method: eliminate stocks unlike past #1s; mix of quantitative screens + qualitative factors; no formula loc.10. Author owns Tesla (disclosure) loc.11.
- Principle: conventional sell-side research aims at beating index modestly, useful only for operating detail on candidates already chosen loc.9 — D.
## loc.13–25 (Ch.1 Forget conventional analysis)
- Analysts' process: EPS forecast x "correct" PE = price target; rating from price vs PT loc.13–14.
- Principle/candidate: market values free cash flow (operating cash flow − capex), not EPS; cites Stern "Earnings per Share Don't Count" FAJ 1974; FCF = OCF − capex (CFI definition) loc.14–16 — B (cites outside article; method via worked hypothetical). Candidate rule: value on FCF not EPS → likely merges with existing FCF-yield rules; check.
- EPS can rise via debt-funded low-return investment while price falls (leverage lowers multiple) loc.14–15 — D/B.
- Beat on EPS can still fall if revenue, gross margin or guidance disappoint loc.17 — D.
- Studies cited: analysts' estimates biased optimistic; 40% of time time-series forecast as accurate as consensus loc.18 — B (no citation detail in text).
- McKinsey 2013 (Koller, Jiang, Raj): no statistically valid evidence lower earnings volatility → higher returns; of 50 lowest-volatility S&P 500 firms <10 diversified; no premium for low earnings volatility loc.19, 21 — B.
- Earnings management: shifting expenses, channel stuffing with discounts, timing write-downs (inventory, receivables, goodwill), acquisition reserves released later, GE quarter-end acquisitions loc.22–24 — C/D. Principle: reported EPS can be managed; check cash flow and receivables/inventory vs sales.
- Sarbanes-Oxley 2002 reduced outright fraud (CEO certification) loc.25 — D.

- EVA (Stewart): capitalise R&D and advertising; Amazon EVA nearly tripled in 5 years to mid-2013 while EPS fell from $2.50 to −$0.33, missed consensus 11 straight quarters, price >4x loc.34 — C/B (one case from Stewart 2013 book).
