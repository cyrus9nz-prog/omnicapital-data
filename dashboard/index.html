How to read a card

The bar under each stock puts today's price (the dark tick) against two ranges: the grey band is where one month of normal volatility could take it (one standard deviation, about two times in three), and the green band is the desk's fair-value range from at least two valuation methods. A price left of the green band means the methods value the stock above its price. Undervalued needs two methods 20% or more above the price.

Big-winner traits count observable features of past big winners (fast revenue growth, widening margins, an earnings beat, price above its 200-day average, beating the S&P 500 over six months). Having them doesn't predict the outcome. "S&P 500 · tested rules" stocks passed screens that were backtested; "Small/mid · untested rules" stocks passed growth and trend rules no one has tested on that group.

Signals: strong buy to sell now

The trader rates every listed stock each day on one scale. A buy opens a paper position and a sell closes it, and each closed position is compared with the S&P 500 over the same days, so the record shows whether the rules work. These are the desk's paper signals under its rules, not orders.

What each rating means

Buy: at least three playbook rules from two different schools fire, an entry rule triggers at today's price, the researcher found nothing disqualifying, and a position fits your risk tier (shares = tier dollars ÷ distance to the stop: up to $15 on the tested S&P 500 lane, up to $10 on the untested small/mid lane).

Strong buy: everything a buy needs, plus an undervalued label on two or more methods, the researcher confirming the call, no red flags, no "hype ahead of evidence" flag, the S&P 500's 50-day average above its 200-day, and four or more rules from three schools (or at least one A-graded rule).

Hold: the default. For a stock with no position it means no entry yet, with the price that would make it a buy when there is one; for an open position it means no exit has triggered.

Sell: an exit by plan (the target is reached, the 20-day time limit or a mixed-indicator bar), or for a stock with no position a bearish lean that playbook rules support. Bearish trades use puts only when the options checks pass.

Sell now: a hard exit: the stop is hit, the trade's invalidation condition is met, the researcher contradicts the call or finds a disqualifying red flag, or, for the tested lane, the S&P 500's 50-day average falls below its 200-day.

Paper positions fill at the trigger level (a buy-stop fills only if a later day trades through it, and is cancelled on a mixed-indicator bar first). If a stop and a target fall inside the same day's range, the stop is assumed. Returns are before costs.

Heat tracker

How hot each stock is, out of 100: 60% substance (playbook fit, value, trend, insider buying, fund flows, catalysts, minus red flags) and 40% buzz (news tone and volume, Reddit, X). A wide gap between the two is flagged. Heat is an untested desk score, not a buy signal; the trader's verdict below is the call.

Heat scores appear after the researcher's first run.

How heat is scored

Substance, 0–100: playbook fit up to 25 (5 per A-graded rule that fired, 3 per B, 2 per C, 1 per D); value up to 20 (undervalued 20, fair 10, can't tell 5, overvalued 0); trend up to 15 (5 each for price above its 200-day average, beating the S&P 500 over six months, and the S&P 500's 50-day average above its 200-day); insiders up to 20 (open-market buying by two or more officers or directors 20, by one 12, none 8, net selling outside pre-set plans 0); funds up to 10 (13F holdings rising 10, flat 5, falling 0); catalysts up to 10 (a dated positive event in the last 90 days, such as a government award or major deal). Minus 10 for each red flag: share sales or an at-the-market program, going-concern doubt, a short-seller report, or insiders and sponsors selling into retail demand.

Buzz, 0–100: news tone up to 40 (from the relevance-weighted sentiment of the past week's articles), news volume up to 20 (past week against the prior month's weekly average), Reddit up to 20 and X up to 20 (none, some, heavy, and whether mostly positive). A source the researcher couldn't read is left out and buzz is scaled over the rest.

Heat = 0.6 × substance + 0.4 × buzz. Hot is 80 and up, warm 60–79, neutral 40–59, cold under 40. "Hype ahead of evidence" means buzz is 30 or more points above substance: attention without support, the setup where people buy the top. "Quiet strength" is the reverse. Each day's scores are kept with the price, so the desk can later check whether heat meant anything.
