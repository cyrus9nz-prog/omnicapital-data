# Piard — Quantitative Investing (2013; revised ebook 2016) (read 2026-11-10, in chat)
## loc.1–13
- loc.2: disclaimer: results are mostly simulations (CFTC 4.41 hypothetical-performance warning). Grading note: a backtest with defined rules, period and sample counts as a systematic test (A) under the desk's scale, flagged "simulated".
- loc.4: all strategies: US stocks and non-leveraged ETFs, very liquid, limit orders recommended, long only; concepts drawn from academic/professional publications.
- loc.5–6: quant investing = measurable hypotheses tested empirically; benefits: no opinions/emotions, reproducible. Risks: wrong criteria; edges decay. D.
- loc.6–8 four anomaly families: (1) market timing — exit an asset class when an indicator (usually a moving average; also put/call, sentiment, % of S&P 100 above 200-day MA, forward EPS) enters a risky zone; works best on broad indexes. (2) momentum — return over 1, 3, 6 or 12 months; ranks assets; works best on indexes and sectors. (3) seasonals — Sell in May, Halloween, Santa Claus; more reliable on diversified indexes than sectors. (4) valuation — intrinsic value (latest quarter), growth history, dividend history. Overview only.
- loc.9–12 metrics: CAGR; drawdown on daily closes (intraday is deeper); max drawdown and max drawdown duration; Sterling = average annual return ÷ (max drawdown + 10%); Sharpe; Sortino (downside deviation only); Kelly K = P − (1−P)/W used as a robustness indicator, not a bet size; K95 uses the 95%-confidence lower bound of P. Definitions.
- loc.12–13 AUTHOR'S STRATEGY ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA (for his own weekly/monthly strategies): ≥10-year simulation; CAGR ≥15%; max drawdown ≤30% and not much above the CAGR; Sortino ≥0.9; Kelly ≥0.2. Combine ≥3 strategies with different rationales, aiming for CAGR >20% and max drawdown <15%. → candidate RULE (process). Evidence: author's own standards, not tested → D.
- loc.12: a 2% difference in average return between strategies may be random; interpret ratios relatively (a 0.8 Sharpe may suit an individual, a day-trader may need >2). D.
- loc.13: the main use of a backtest is to measure risk (drawdown depth and length, robustness ratios), not to predict returns. D.
## loc.14–26
- loc.14: drawdown arithmetic: recovery gain = x/(1−x) (30% → 43%, 50% → 100%); marginal cost 1/(1−x)² (an extra 1% at 50% drawdown needs ~4% more gain). Arithmetic shown.
- loc.14–15: requirements for a credible backtest: wide US universe, reliable end-of-day data, 10+ years of history, dead companies kept (no survivorship bias), time-stamped fundamentals (no look-ahead), slippage and commissions. Uses Portfolio123. D (method requirements).
- loc.15–16: conventions: decisions on the prior close's data, orders at next open, dividends reinvested; weekly or 4-week rebalancing.
- loc.17–20 SPY MARKET TIMING TEST (2000-01-01 → 2013-01-01, 13 y, daily, no costs): buy & hold CAGR 1.8% / max DD −55.4%; price > SMA200: 2.7% / −24.2%; SMA50 > SMA200: 5.8% / −17.0% (only 15 trades in 13 y); 2× leverage 10.7% / −31.7%. Since 2009 the SMA50/200 version lagged buy & hold in a trending market (false signals) but kept drawdown and volatility lower. Weekly checks ≈ daily (11.0% / −33.0% at 2×); 4-week checks worse and start-date sensitive (8.0–10.1%, DD −35 to −39.5%). With 2% borrowing + 0.1% costs, weekly 2×: 9.4% / −34%. → RULE (A, simulated).
- loc.20: Faber (2009) 1900–2008 S&P 500 with dividends: 10-month SMA timing CAGR 10.5% / DD −50.3% / Sharpe 0.54 vs buy & hold 9.2% / −83.7% / 0.29 → B (outside study). Leverage would not have survived 1929. Main benefit is shallower drawdown, not return.
- loc.21–22 FIVE US INDEX ETFs (DIA, SPY, QQQ, MDY, IWM), 2000–2013, weekly, 0.1% costs: buy & hold 3.8% / −54.2%; each ETF timed on its own SMA50/200: 3.9% / −26.3%; ALL timed on the S&P 500's SMA50/200: 7.0% / −17.9%. Global filter beat individual. A (simulated).
- loc.22: these are variants of the same bet — don't count them as separate strategies when sizing (Kelly). 
- loc.22–24 ELEVEN SECTOR ETFs, 2001-06 → 2013-01: sectors buy & hold 5.3% / −55.7%; individual timing 6.3% / −17.3%; global (S&P 500 filter) 8.2% / −17.5%; combined (S&P 500 filter AND each sector's own SMA50 > SMA200, equal weight among those passing) 8.6% / −17.0%. A (simulated).
- loc.24–26 SEVEN GLOBAL ASSET ETFs (7–10y and 20y+ Treasuries, S&P 500, Europe, Latin America, Pacific ex-Japan, US REITs), 2003-06 → 2013-01 (9.5 y), each held only while its own SMA50 > SMA200, weekly: capped at 1/7 each: CAGR 11.2% / DD −11.4% (Sortino 0.84, Kelly 0.20, longest DD 90 weeks) vs buy & hold 11.9% / −45.8%; uncapped (equal weight among those passing) 14.5% / −18.0%; 2× capped 21.0% / −21.9% (2% borrow, 0.1% costs). Faber 1973–2008 similar model: >11% CAGR, DD <10% (B). → RULE (A, simulated; short 9.5-year window).
## loc.27–39
- loc.27 S&P 500 STOCKS (each capped 1/500), 2000–2013, weekly, 0.1% costs: equal-weight buy & hold 7.4% / −57.9%; each stock on its own SMA50/200: 5.3% / −20.7%; all on the S&P 500 filter: 8.2% / −22.4%; combined: 6.4% / −16.3%. Global filter: best return; combined: lowest drawdown. A (simulated). Supports the index filter for stock portfolios.
- loc.28–30 SECTOR ROTATION (11 sector ETFs, combined market timing): keep top 6 by momentum: 20-day 9.8% / −16.0%; 60-, 120-, 200-day all 8.1–8.9% (no cherry-picking: all plain intervals). Top N with 20-day: N = 4–11 all 8.8–9.8%; N = 1–3 worse (0.3–7.7%). ADD 7–10y TREASURY ETF when the S&P 500's SMA50 ≤ SMA200 (2002-08 → 2013-01, 0.1% costs): CAGR 12.3% vs 9.3% without, DD −16.2% both, Sortino 0.79 vs 0.47, longest DD 62 vs 88 weeks. Each pick still needs its own SMA50 > SMA200 (incl. the bond ETF); equal weight among those passing, no cap. 4-week rebalancing: worst start date CAGR 10.5%, DD −22.5%. → RULE. A (simulated, ~10.4 y).
- loc.30–32 GLOBAL ASSET ROTATION (same 7 ETFs, each capped 1/N, also needs its own SMA50 > SMA200): top 6 by momentum: 120-day best (13.3% / −11.8%); 60–200 days all beat the timing-only version. Top N at 120 days: top 1 21.6% / −28.3%; top 3 16.4% / −19.1%; top 5 15.3% / −13.7% (Sortino 0.96, Kelly 0.20, longest DD 88 weeks); 2× top 5: 27.7% / −26.2%; 4-week rebalancing at 2×: worst start 25.5% CAGR, DD −31%. → RULE (supersedes the plain 7-asset timing as the main global-asset rule). A (simulated, ~9.6 y).
- loc.32–35 PAIRED SWITCHING: each 4 weeks hold whichever of an equity-index ETF or long Treasuries (20y+) had the higher 60-day return; 0.1% costs; 2002-08 → 2013-01: Dow ETF 10.7% / −16.9%; S&P 500 ETF 12.2% / −17.7%; Nasdaq-100 ETF 12.6% / −32.0%; mid-cap 400 ETF 18.1% / −22.9% (Sortino 1.12; 4 start dates 17.8–22%, DD 18–27%; vs buy & hold 9.9% / −55.4% and Treasuries 8.1% / −27%); Russell 2000 ETF 16.7% / −23.7%. Emerging-markets ETF vs Treasuries (2003-05 → 2013-02, 4 start dates): 20.3–24.1%, DD −23.9 to −31.1% (EM buy & hold 16.2% / −64.3%). Use Treasuries, not emerging-market debt (positively correlated with stocks). Avoid single-sector ETFs for switching. TRIPLE SWITCH (mid-cap, EM, Treasuries): 25.3–29.1%, DD −23.9 to −30.9%, Sortino 1.31–1.50; longest DD 21 months; P(gain per 4 weeks) 68.5%, P95 60%; Kelly 44% (27% on P95). Author: works as long as stocks and bonds stay negatively correlated. → RULE. A (simulated, ~10.5 y, start-date tested).
- loc.36 NEGATIVE RESULT: top 100 S&P 500 stocks by momentum (with combined timing, 1% cap, 0.2% costs), 2001-06 → 2013-01: every momentum length lagged the 500-stock timing benchmark (6.2%): 200-day 4.8%, 120-day 3.4%, 60-day 2.7%, 20-day 0.7% — the shorter, the worse. A (simulated). Caution for single-stock momentum ranking.
- loc.37–38 SEASONALS: Halloween effect (in Nov–Apr, out May–Oct). Jacobsen & Zhang (2012): data since 1693, 108 countries; winter beats summer, worldwide, gap widened recently → B (outside study). Monthly table: September negative in all four periods (1693–2009, 1929–2011, 1961–2011, 1999–2012); January effect failed 1999–2012. US had one of the best winter gains among developed markets.
- loc.38–39: DJIA 1950–2011: invested only May–Oct would have lost ~10% cumulatively with major drawdowns; Nov–Apr captured the gains (author's own season-end series; appendix). A/B.
- loc.39: 26 of 108 countries show the inverse (better summers). Two-season strategy uses Singapore + Brazil country ETFs, Nov 1 → Apr 30, cash otherwise (results next chunk).
## loc.40–51
- loc.40: Singapore + Brazil ETFs Nov 1 → Apr 30, cash otherwise, 2001-08 → 2013: CAGR 14.85%, DD −24.3%, Sortino 0.48; all 12-month periods between summers positive for 11 years; holding both all year: 16.6% / −63.2%. - loc.41–43 FOUR-SEASON: stock index Mar–Apr and Oct–Dec; long Treasuries Jan–Feb and May–Sep; 2002-08 → 2013-01, 0.1% costs. With S&P 500 ETF 17.6% / −35.5% / Sortino 0.82; Dow 17.8% / −31.8%; Nasdaq-100 20.3% / −35.0% / 0.96; Germany 27.2% / −38.4% / 1.08. ## loc.52–63
- loc.52–53 LUCK FACTOR: weighted mix (5,2,1): 501 weekly returns, win/loss 1.02, P 61.6%, P95 57.2% → Kelly 0.24 falls to 0.16; slight overrating of P widens the spread of possible equity paths a lot. Ten years of tests on documented anomalies = an edge, not a guarantee. A (calculation shown).
- loc.53–54: fundamental models: S&P 500 only, few rules, free screeners; what matters is anticipating institutional money, not "true value".
- loc.54–56 VALUE MODEL: S&P 500 members; price/book (last quarter) < 8; price/cash flow per share (TTM) < 10; P/E excl. extraordinary items (TTM) < 20; average analyst recommendation ≤ 3 (hold or better); rank by lowest total debt/equity; hold top N equal weight; rebalance every 4 weeks; 0.2% costs; market filter = EPS-estimate rule below. 1999–2013: top 6 CAGR 14.6% / DD −28.5% / Sortino 0.64; top 10 14.0% / −33.2%; top 20 12.1% / −32.8%; S&P 500 ETF 3.0% / −55.4%. ~25% of S&P passes; turnover 19% per rebalance. Author: 4–8 positions often a sweet spot. → RULE. A (simulated, 14 y).
- loc.56–57 GROWTH MODEL: S&P 500; EPS change last quarter vs previous quarter > 5%; sales change last quarter vs same quarter a year earlier > 5%; price/cash flow (quarterly) < 8; RSI(14) < 70; rank by lowest debt/equity; top 4, 25% cap; 4-week rebalance; same filter. 1999–2013: top 4 20.0% / −25.3% / Sortino 0.92; top 6 15.2% / −26.6%; top 10 13.3% / −29.1%. Only ~18 stocks passed at writing; turnover 33%. → RULE. A (simulated, 14 y; very few names).
- loc.57–58 DIVIDEND MODEL: S&P 500 excl. utilities; 5-year average dividend yield > 4%; current yield > 4%; short interest/float < 5%; rank by lowest debt/equity; top 10, 10% cap; 4-week rebalance; same filter. 2001–2013: 14.3% / −17.3% / Sortino 0.89 (value 15.9% / −28.5%; growth 18.5% / −25.3%; S&P 500 3.0% / −55.4%). Utilities excluded (concentration; they lowered results). Turnover 12%. → RULE. A (simulated, 12 y).
- loc.58–59 EPS-ESTIMATE MARKET FILTER: sell all stocks when the S&P 500's current-year EPS estimate falls below its value 15 weeks earlier; buy back when strictly above; checked every 4 weeks. Without it the three models still made 12–17% but drew down 55–65%. Timing vs hedging (equal short in the S&P 500 ETF, 2% borrow), 2001–2013: value N 16.3% / −57.0%, hedged 11.9% / −27.1%, timed 15.9% / −28.5%; growth N 13.8% / −64.0%, H 9.1% / −35.5%, T 18.5% / −25.3%; dividend N 12.5% / −59.2%, H 6.4% / −28.0%, T 14.3% / −17.3%. Timing beat hedging on return and Sortino for all three. → RULE (input: S&P 500 consensus EPS history — not connected yet). A (simulated).
- loc.59–60 MIX 50% dividend / 25% value / 25% growth, 2001–2013: CAGR 16%, DD −10.4% (on 4-week closes; daily would be worse), DD duration 19 months, Kelly 0.54 (0.40 on P95). A with caveat.

- loc.61: start from documented anomalies (reversal, continuation, habits/calendar, rhythm) in academic/professional research (e.g. SSRN). D.

## loc.64–74
- loc.64: limit liquidity risk with a minimum average daily volume, a minimum market cap, or an index universe. COSTS: "4-2" rule (buy Dow stocks after 4 down days, sell 2 days later), 14 years: ~16%/yr with zero costs → negative with 0.1% costs. A (simulated).
- loc.64–65: short selling is asymmetric (borrow can be recalled, loss unlimited, forced buy-ins); can't be backtested reliably (no borrow-availability history); 2008 Volkswagen squeeze ~€200 → ~€1,000 in two days. C.
- loc.65–66: keep strategies simple and portable across platforms; fundamental data differ by provider — spot-check across sources. D.
- loc.66–67 OVERFITTING CHECKS: ≥100 decision points; bull, bear and sideways periods covered; outperformance across a broad range of holdings (4–15); stable across start dates; vary each parameter one at a time and reject sharp discontinuities; don't start a strategy while it sits in its longest historical drawdown. D (method).
- loc.67: accepting a bad strategy (alpha risk) is worse than missing a good one; judge drawdown before return; author accepts individual drawdowns up to 35% if the combined portfolio's drawdown stays well below its average return (vs 30% in loc.13). D.
- loc.68: track live vs simulated results; quarantine a strategy whose divergence can't be explained. D.
- loc.68–69: leveraged daily ETFs suffer beta slippage (+25% then −20% leaves the asset flat but a perfect 2× fund at 0.9); path-dependent, so backtests on them need luck; fit for day trading. A (arithmetic).
- loc.69–70: few traders know the real probabilities behind patterns like double tops or doji candles; models are correlations, not causes; avoid "it's different now". D.
